Phishing Awareness Training

Learn how to recognize, avoid and report phishing attacks.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick a victim into revealing sensitive information, opening a malicious attachment, or clicking a harmful link.

Common Warning Signs of Phishing

  • Urgent or threatening messages that pressure you to act immediately.
  • Unexpected requests for passwords, payment details or personal information.
  • Suspicious sender addresses or domains that imitate legitimate organizations.
  • Links whose destination does not match the organization they claim to represent.
  • Unexpected attachments or unusual file types.
  • Spelling, grammar or formatting mistakes that seem unusual for the sender.

How to Recognize Fake Websites

Check before you trust:
  • Inspect the website address carefully.
  • Look for misspelled or misleading domain names.
  • Do not rely only on the padlock icon as proof that a site is legitimate.
  • For banking or important accounts, navigate to the official website yourself instead of following an unexpected link.

Social Engineering Tactics

Attackers may use psychological pressure to influence their victims.

  • Urgency: “Act now or your account will be closed.”
  • Authority: Pretending to be a manager, bank or government agency.
  • Fear: Threatening consequences if the victim does not respond.
  • Curiosity: Using surprising messages or attachments to encourage a click.
  • Reward: Promising prizes, money or other benefits.

Best Practices

  • Think before clicking links or opening unexpected attachments.
  • Verify unusual requests through a separate trusted communication channel.
  • Use strong, unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Keep operating systems, browsers and security software updated.
  • Report suspicious messages to your organization or email provider.
  • Never share passwords or verification codes through unexpected messages.

Conclusion
